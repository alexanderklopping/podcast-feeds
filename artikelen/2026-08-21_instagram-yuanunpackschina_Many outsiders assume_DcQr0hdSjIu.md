## Transcript

China does have media watchdogs, just not in the way most outsiders imagine. Recently, a negative case broke out in Zhejiang province. A government official and a businessman were accused of molesting a young woman. The case was widely reported in China, but what really pushed it out of the national trending list was official media from other provinces. Yes, Chinese media is regulated, but there is an interesting pattern. Local official media may be cautious about criticizing their own province or city, but they can be very aggressive when reporting problems somewhere else. Zhejiang media has reported negative stories from Shandong, Henan, and Hedongjiang. So this time, when Zhejiang had its own scandal, other provinces returned their favor. The official and the businessman have now both been criminally detained, and cases where public pressure calls officials their positions are not rare in China. Chinese provinces compete intensively for companies' talent investment and even reputation. So while China is highly centralized politically, many parts of the system are more competitive and decentralized than outsiders imagine. Did this kind of media supervision surprise you?
